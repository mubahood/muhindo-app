# Course 24 ⭐ — Working With AI

**Tier 1 · Foundations · Level: Beginner · Prerequisites: none · TOP FEATURED**

A practical course in using AI assistants for real work, for anybody: a student
writing a dissertation, a farmer pricing a harvest, an accountant, a teacher, a
developer. It is not about the tools, which change every few months. It is about
asking well, and about the harder half nobody teaches, which is knowing when the
answer is wrong.

**What you will learn**

- Get useful work out of an AI assistant instead of vague answers
- Check a claim, a number or a piece of code before you rely on it
- Do a task that takes you two hours in twenty minutes, safely
- Recognise the situations where using AI will damage your work or your name

---

## Module 1 — What you are actually talking to

1. **What an AI assistant is, without the mathematics** — a system that predicts likely text, which explains almost everything it does well and everything it does badly.
2. **Why it sounds certain when it is wrong** — confidence is a property of the writing style, not of the truth. The single most important idea in this course.
3. **What it can and cannot see** — your document, your screen, the internet, your past chats. Knowing which of these applies changes what you should ask.
4. **Pick your assistant and set it up** — one free option and one paid, on a phone and on a laptop, and what each does better.

## Module 2 — Asking well

5. **Give it the context it needs** — the same question with and without background, compared side by side on one real task.
6. **Say what you want back** — length, format, tone, audience. A request without these gets an essay when you wanted a list.
7. **Show it an example** — one sample of the output you want beats three paragraphs describing it.
8. **Ask it to work step by step** — for anything involving reasoning or arithmetic, this alone removes most errors.
9. **Push back and iterate** — the second and third message are where the value is. Most people stop at the first answer.
10. **Rewrite a weak prompt into a strong one** — a graded exercise. You are given five poor requests and improve each one.

## Module 3 — Checking the answer

11. **Decide how much it matters** — a birthday message and a medical dosage do not deserve the same scrutiny. A simple test for how hard to check.
12. **Verify a fact or a number** — find the original source. What to do when it cites something that does not exist.
13. **Verify a calculation** — do one row by hand. If the sample is right the method is usually right; if it is wrong nothing else matters.
14. **Verify a piece of code you cannot fully read** — run it on a case where you already know the answer.
15. **Spot a fabricated citation** — what invented references look like, and why they are common in academic work.
16. **Ask it to argue against itself** — asking for the strongest objection to its own answer surfaces weaknesses it will not volunteer.
17. **Know what you must never accept unverified** — figures in a report, legal or medical claims, anything you sign your name to.

## Module 4 — Real work, done faster

18. **Write a first draft you then rewrite** — a cover letter, a proposal, a complaint. The draft is scaffolding, not the building.
19. **Summarise something long** — a 40-page report, a contract, a set of lecture notes, with the questions that make a summary useful.
20. **Clean up messy data** — a list of names in five formats, phone numbers with and without country codes, dates that do not agree.
21. **Translate between English, Luganda and Swahili** — where it is good, where it flattens meaning, and why you check anything you would not say yourself.
22. **Learn a subject with no teacher** — how to be taught by an assistant: ask for explanations at your level, then ask it to test you.
23. **Prepare for an interview or a viva** — have it play the panel and ask you the hard questions.
24. **Turn a task you repeat into a saved prompt** — write it once, reuse it every week.

## Module 5 — Where it will cost you

25. **Confident fabrication, seen up close** — ask about something obscure you know well and watch it invent. Everybody should do this once.
26. **What not to paste in** — patient records, student data, salaries, contracts under NDA, national ID numbers. Whose server it lands on and why that matters.
27. **Academic honesty** — the difference between using AI to understand and using it to submit. Where the line sits at a Ugandan university and what happens at a viva when you cannot defend your own words.
28. **Work you cannot stand behind** — the test that if you could not explain it to somebody who asked, you should not send it.
29. **Bias and blind spots** — why an assistant trained mostly on American and European text gives thin answers about East African law, agriculture and business, and how to compensate.

## Module 6 — Making it yours

30. **Build your own prompt library** — the six or seven you will use for years, kept somewhere you can find them.
31. **Decide what you will not delegate** — the parts of your work that are yours because the judgement is the job.
32. **A weekly workflow** — where the assistant sits in a normal week without becoming the thing you cannot work without.

## Final project

Take one task from your own week that currently takes you two hours or more.
Do it with an AI assistant. Submit three things: the finished work, the prompts
you used, and an honest account of **what the assistant got wrong and how you
caught it**.

That last part is the assessment. Producing output is easy and everybody will
manage it; the course is teaching judgement, and judgement is only visible in
the errors you found.

**Quiz ideas:** given an answer and a context, decide how hard to verify it ·
spot the fabricated citation among four · which of these five would you paste
into an assistant and which would you not · rewrite a weak prompt · true or
false on academic use.

**Continue to:** Course 02 (AI-Powered Web Development) if you write code, or
Course 22 (Microsoft Office Mastery) to use this on documents and spreadsheets
every day.

## Decisions

- Module 3 is the longest and sits before the productivity module on purpose.
  Teaching someone to generate faster before teaching them to check is how
  confident wrong work gets produced at scale.
- No product names in lesson titles. The tools will move; the lesson about
  checking a calculation will not. Setup in lesson 4 names two current options
  and is expected to be re-recorded roughly yearly, alone.
- Written for a non-programmer throughout. Lesson 14 is the only one touching
  code and is framed so a non-coder can still follow the principle.
- No video links: nothing on the channel covers general AI use. Every lesson
  here is a fresh recording.
